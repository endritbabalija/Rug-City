# Games to Learn From — Beyond Catizen

Oct 11, 2026 · @endrit

## At a glance

The closest models for Who Rugged? are not crypto games but group games: Werewolf on Telegram proves the format, Jackbox shows how groups pay, and the crypto games mostly teach what to avoid.

| Game | Type | How it makes money | Lesson for Who Rugged? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Werewolf for Telegram | Group hidden-role game in chat | Free bot; newer versions add an in-game currency | The group chat plus private-clue format already works on Telegram |
| Jackbox Party Packs | Party games played on phones | One-time pack purchase by the host | Let one host unlock a case pack for the whole group |
| Among Us | Social deduction | Cosmetics | Drama is the marketing; make reveals easy to share |
| Wordle | Daily puzzle | Sold to The New York Times | A spoiler-free result card spreads the game |
| HQ Trivia | Live quiz with cash prizes | Sponsors, investor money | Prizes must come from revenue, paid fast |
| AI Dungeon | AI storytelling | Monthly subscriptions | Charge for the costly AI, use the cheapest model that works |
| Catizen | Idle merge game on Telegram | In-app purchases, selling traffic to partners, CATI token | Keep paid speed-ups and the sponsor storefront; don't rely on airdrop hopes |
| Notcoin | Tap-to-earn, then platform | Projects pay in NOT to reach players | A big audience can be rented to sponsors |
| Hamster Kombat | Tap-to-earn | Token airdrop | Unfair payouts and bots kill a game overnight |
| Pixels | Farming game | In-app purchases plus a rewards engine | Rewards must stay below revenue |

## Telegram and crypto games

Four games show the full arc: Catizen built three money engines around a token, Notcoin turned an airdrop into a platform, Hamster Kombat showed how a botched airdrop kills a game, and Pixels showed rewards only survive when revenue pays for them.

**Catizen: three money engines**

- **What it is:** an idle merge game where you combine cats into stronger cats that earn coins while you are away. Open beta launched on 19 March 2024 ([Binance Research](https://www.binance.com/research/projects/catizen)).
- **Engine 1, in-app purchases:** players paid for boosts and faster progress in TON, USDT, NOT and other crypto. By mid-September 2024 it reported $31M of revenue from 1.1M paying players, roughly $28 each ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/catizen-clicker-game-revenue-reaches-31-million/)).
- **Engine 2, selling traffic:** a Game Center and Launchpool put other games and token projects in front of its players. The app center claimed 7M daily users, and its game Bombie drew 1.3M users in three weeks ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/316195/catizen-completes-business-model-upgrade-pioneering-the-development-of-telegram-applications)).
- **Engine 3, the CATI token:** in-game points decided airdrop shares, so spending felt like investing. Team and treasury held 35% of supply, and half of app-center revenue was pledged to token buybacks ([Binance Research](https://www.binance.com/research/projects/catizen), [BlockTempo](https://www.blocktempo.com/?p=381232)).
- **What happened:** CATI listed at $1.29 on 20 September 2024 and traded around $0.04–0.06 by mid-2026, about 95% lower ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/catizen/)). Catizen has since rebranded around AI cats and a broader game platform.
- **Lesson:** keep the paid speed-ups and the sponsor storefront. Don't make spending depend on airdrop hopes, because that reason to pay disappears once the token launches.
- **Full breakdown:** Catizen — Business Model Deep Dive

**Notcoin: from tap game to traffic platform**

- **What it was:** tap a coin to mine points. About 35M players could claim NOT in the May 2024 airdrop ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/232647/notcoin-touches-1-billion-market-cap-after-earnings-missions-launch?amp=1)).
- **The model after the airdrop:** no new tokens are printed. Other projects buy NOT and use it to reward players for trying their apps, so Notcoin becomes paid promotion ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/225034/notcoin-telegram-game-return-after-token-launch-big-changes)).
- **The ambition:** its founder wanted it to be "the Netflix of social, viral games", a launchpad for other studios ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/237334/telegram-game-notcoin-teases-fresh-start-lost-dogs?amp=1)).
- **Lesson:** a big audience is an asset you can rent to others. Sponsored Bounty Cases are your version.

**Hamster Kombat: what not to do**

- **What happened:** about 300M players at the peak, down to 40.9M a month after a disputed airdrop. Only 131M players received tokens and 2.3M were banned for cheating ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/hamster-kombat-loses-nearly-260-million-players-post-listing/)).
- **Lesson:** when players are only there for the payout, they leave the day it lands. Clear rules and bot protection matter from day one.

**Pixels: rewards paid by revenue**

- **What it is:** a farming game on Ronin that reported over $20M of 2024 revenue ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/podcast/can-play-to-earn-ever-be-sustainable/)).
- **The fix:** in 2024 it paid out more in rewards than it earned, so it moved to tracking return on reward spend and targets $1.10–1.50 of revenue per $1 of rewards ([BlockchainGamer.biz](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/features/interviews/41204/), [Pixels AMA](https://pixelspost.substack.com/p/ama-recap-nov-5-2025)).
- **Lesson:** this is exactly the rule behind your season prize pool. Rewards come out of revenue, never ahead of it.

## Group and party games

These are Who Rugged?'s real ancestors: hidden roles, private information and arguing in a group. They prove the format works, and Jackbox shows how to charge for it.

**Werewolf for Telegram: the closest relative**

- **How it plays:** a bot runs the game in a group chat and sends each player their secret role by private message. The group discusses and votes publicly in the chat ([Shopee guide](https://shopee.sg/blog/telegram-game-bots-group-chat/), [ProfesionalReview](https://www.profesionalreview.com/2017/05/11/los-mejores-bots-juegos-telegram/)).
- **Why it matters:** it has run on Telegram for years with the same mechanic you plan: public group chat plus private clues by DM. Proof the format fits Telegram.
- **The modern version:** a Discord Werewolf bot now adds AI narration for every death, narrator voice packs, AI bot players to fill empty seats, and an in-game currency earned by playing ([top.gg](https://top.gg/bot/1510711904543182970)).
- **Lesson:** AI bot players solve the "not enough friends online" problem. Voice packs are a cheap cosmetic to sell.

**Jackbox Party Packs: paid content for groups**

- **The model:** one person buys a pack of five party games for about $25–30, and friends join free from their phones ([SteamPulse](https://steampulse.org/game/331670)).
- **Proof it sells:** sales jumped up to 1,000% in early 2020 when people needed games to play together remotely, and Party Pack 7's first-day sales were five times Pack 6's ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/jackbox-games-200-million-players-bloomberg-report/)).
- **Lesson:** in a group game, one host pays and everyone plays. Let the host unlock a case pack for the whole group. One sale then brings in several players.

**Among Us: the social-deduction breakout**

- **What it shows:** a simple "find the traitor" game became a global hit through streamers watching friends accuse each other. It earned from cosmetics, not power.
- **Lesson:** the drama is the marketing. Make the funniest accusations and reveals easy to clip and share.

## Live and daily formats

HQ Trivia shows how a prize-driven live event dies when prizes outrun revenue. Wordle shows how one shareable daily result can spread a game with no marketing at all.

**HQ Trivia: the prize trap**

- **What it was:** a live mobile quiz twice a day where everyone who answered all 12 questions split a cash prize ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/hq-trivia-shuts-down-leaving-us-with-more-questions-than-answers/)). Peak audiences passed 1M, with sponsors such as General Motors and Nike ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/hq-trivia-shuts-down-as-investors-withdraw-support/572421/)).
- **What broke:** prizes were funded by investors rather than revenue. When too many people won, each got pennies: one February 2018 game split its prize among 9,000+ winners at 23 cents each, and cheating software made it worse ([Goodreads summary](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22109040-what-happened-to-hq-trivia?tab=book)). Late payouts drove players away.
- **The end:** investors stopped funding it in February 2020. The final prize was $5 split among 523 players ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/hq-trivia-shuts-down-as-investors-withdraw-support/572421/)).
- **Lessons:** fund prizes from revenue and sponsors, never your own runway; reward the best, not everyone who finishes; pay out fast and reliably; plan for cheating from day one.

**Wordle: the shareable result**

- **What it was:** one free five-letter puzzle a day. Players shared their result as a grid of colored squares that showed how they did without spoiling the answer ([NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/the-new-york-times-is-buying-wordle-the-game-that-exploded-in-popularity-this-month/)).
- **Growth:** 90 players on 1 November 2021, a global craze by January 2022, then bought by The New York Times for a low seven-figure sum ([NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/the-new-york-times-is-buying-wordle-the-game-that-exploded-in-popularity-this-month/)).
- **Lesson:** design the verdict card like Wordle's grid: spoiler-free, instantly readable and a bit of a brag.

## AI-native games

AI Dungeon proves people pay monthly for AI storytelling, and also that AI costs can sink a game unless pricing is built around them from the start.

**AI Dungeon (Latitude)**

- **What it is:** an AI writes an endless adventure that reacts to whatever the player types. It started as a hackathon project and launched in 2019 ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/ru/news/Generative_AI/item-18179)).
- **The cost problem:** every AI reply costs money. Latitude reported about half a cent per request across a couple of million requests a day, and its bills later fell to under $100,000 a month ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/03/13/chatgpt-and-generative-ai-are-booming-but-at-a-very-expensive-price.html/)).
- **The fixes:** cheaper models tuned for the job, and a mix of smaller models that matched bigger ones on quality ([AI21 case study](https://www.ai21.com/blog/latitude-case-study)).
- **The model:** free to try, with subscriptions from $9.99 to $49.99 a month for better models, longer memory and image credits ([Latitude](https://latitudegames.notion.site/Membership-Plan-Overview-9085c85b381a4e29b6caf644b66c6382)).
- **Lessons:** charge for the AI that costs most, such as extra interrogations; use the cheapest model that is good enough; pre-generate what can be reused. Your case-generation design already does all three.

## What to borrow for Who Rugged?

Eight changes come out of these games; the first four are cheap and could go into the next build.

- [ ] **Host unlocks for the group** (Jackbox): one player buys a case pack and the whole group plays it.
- [ ] **AI bot detectives fill empty seats** (Discord Werewolf): a case can start with two friends plus AI teammates.
- [ ] **Spoiler-free verdict card** (Wordle): a small emoji grid of the group's accuracy and time, easy to paste into any chat.
- [ ] **Narrator voice packs** (Discord Werewolf): noir, degen or corporate voices as a cheap cosmetic to sell for Stars.
- [ ] **Rent the audience** (Notcoin, Catizen): sponsors pay in their own token or in TON to run a branded case.
- [ ] **Prize pool from revenue only** (Pixels, HQ Trivia): keep the season pool at a fixed share of revenue, rank-weighted, paid out fast.
- [ ] **Anti-cheat from day one** (Hamster Kombat, HQ Trivia): one account per Telegram ID, rate limits, and no points for unfinished cases.
- [ ] **Clip-ready moments** (Among Us): a "best accusation" replay screen designed to be shared.
